# Data sources and methods

Generated by `python -m pipeline build`. Do not edit by hand.

## How each column is built

| Column | Method |
|---|---|
| `average_salary` | GNI per capita (Atlas, US$), log-scaled. |
| `cost_of_living` | Consumer price level = household-consumption PPP factor ÷ exchange rate (1.0 = US prices), used when both are from years at most 1 apart; otherwise the economy-wide level (GDP per capita US$ ÷ PPP $). Higher = more expensive; the app inverts it for 'affordability'. Reflects what residents pay: expat rents in hubs like Singapore, Hong Kong and Dubai are much higher than this suggests. |
| `job_market` | 60% low vulnerable (informal / own-account) employment + 40% low unemployment. Unemployment alone flatters economies with large informal sectors. |
| `safety` | 50% low homicide rate (log-scaled) + 50% Global Peace Index (conflict, militarisation, political instability). |
| `healthcare` | 60% WHO universal health coverage index + 40% life expectancy. |
| `internet` | Share of the population using the internet. Measures access, not speed. |
| `english` | Native English countries = 1.0. Others: EF EPI score robust-scaled to 0.05–0.90. |
| `language_barrier` | 1 − english (kept for compatibility; it carries no independent information). |
| `climate` | Comfort of the main city's mean annual temperature: 1.0 at 21 °C, falling to 0 at 7 °C (colder) or 41 °C (hotter); heat is penalised half as fast as cold. |
| `pr_ease` | Editorial judgement from the original dataset (no public index exists). |
| `salary_usd_month` | Editorial estimate of average monthly net salary (no open source with global coverage). |
| `rent_usd_month` | Editorial estimate of 1-bed city-centre rent (no open source with global coverage). |

Scaling: each indicator is clipped to its 5th–95th percentile across the dataset's countries and min-max scaled to 0–1, so one extreme country can't squash everyone else.

## ML gap filling

Iterative imputation (MICE) with Bayesian ridge regression, standardized indicators, 5-fold cross-validation per indicator. A predicted value is used only when its indicator's cross-validated R² is at least 0.5 (1.0 = perfect, 0 = no better than the average).
